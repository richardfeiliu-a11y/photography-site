# UX Principles

These ten principles are review rules for future IA, prototype, visual design, and implementation work.

1. **Photography wins the attention contest.** Archive controls, metadata, identity, and navigation may support the work but must not become the visual subject of a Gallery.

2. **Editorial intent defines a Gallery.** Never create or split a Gallery automatically because a trip, Visit, shoot, folder, date bucket, or import batch exists. A publishable Gallery can explain why its Photos belong together, why the order matters, and where the work ends.

3. **One object, multiple discovery paths.** Home, Archive, and Places must point to one canonical Gallery; never duplicate content to make the navigation look symmetrical.

4. **Discovery may be denser than viewing.** Archive and Places can show covers, dates, counts, and grouping; that density must recede once the visitor enters a Gallery.

5. **Use photographed time for history and publication time for recency.** Backfilled work belongs to when it was photographed, while Home may still identify it as a recent addition.

6. **Place connects work; Visit does not organize the interface.** A Place may aggregate Galleries across decades, but no public trip/Visit layer is introduced without a demonstrated user task.

7. **Sequence is primary; overview restores orientation.** Preserve the photographer's order while giving visitors a quiet way to understand or jump within a long Gallery.

8. **Mobile behavior is intentional.** Use vertical progress, preserve mixed orientations, prevent horizontal overflow, avoid hover-only controls, and make continuation touch- and keyboard-accessible.

9. **Every Gallery has a clear ending.** The visitor must be able to continue to the next work, return to Archive, or follow a relevant Place without reaching a dead end.

10. **Complexity must earn its maintenance cost.** Search arrives with retrieval need; Map, tags, generic facets, equipment UI, and speculative entities remain absent until a documented problem justifies them.
